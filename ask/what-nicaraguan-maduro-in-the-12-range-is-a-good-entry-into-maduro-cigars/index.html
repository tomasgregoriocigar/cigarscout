What Nicaraguan maduro in the $12 range is a good entry into maduro cigars?

Answered by Cubichi, TrustedCigar's cigar expert, from the verified price catalogue. Prices are per stick, with the retailer and the check date named. Published November 14, 2026.

Ask Cubichi · Answered November 14, 2026

Short answer: the Drew Estate Undercrown Maduro. It is a Nicaraguan cigar built around a dark Mexican San Andres maduro wrapper, and it gives a first-time maduro smoker the full picture, cocoa, baking spice, and natural sweetness, without the harsh edge cheaper dark cigars can carry. Every size we track verifies under $12 a stick.

The line has a story worth knowing. Undercrown was blended at the Drew Estate factory in Esteli for the rollers themselves, the crew behind Liga Privada, who wanted a rich maduro they could smoke every day. The catalogue profile reads: Nicaragua, Mexican San Andres maduro wrapper, Connecticut Habano binder, Brazilian Mata Fina and Nicaraguan Habano filler, medium-full to full in strength. Rich, yes. Punishing, no. That is exactly where an entry into maduro should start.

Verified prices, per stick: Robusto, Neptune Cigar $8.75 (single, checked October 30, 2026) and Best Cigar Prices $8.40 (box of 25, checked November 13, 2026). Gran Toro, Neptune Cigar $9.45 (single, checked October 30, 2026) and Best Cigar Prices $9.04 (box of 25, checked November 13, 2026). Toro, Best Cigar Prices $9.40 (box of 10, checked November 13, 2026). In stock at both retailers on the dates checked.

Start with the Robusto as a single from Neptune before you commit to a box. If the dark side agrees with you, the Gran Toro by the box at Best Cigar Prices is the value move. Neptune Cigar and Best Cigar Prices are both Approved retailers on the Retailer Trust Index.
